ESPN makes $108 million Joe Buck decision as veteran announcer secures long term future with Monday Night Football
Joe Buck’s future at ESPN now looks much clearer. The veteran broadcaster has reportedly agreed to a six-year contract extension worth about $108 million, according to The Athletic. The deal would keep him as the voice of Monday Night Football through 2032 and also cover ESPN’s next two Super Bowl broadcasts in 2027 and 2031. The move comes months after Buck said he wanted to finish his broadcasting career with ESPN.

Joe Buck reportedly gets $108 million ESPN extension

Buck joined ESPN in 2022 after leaving FOX, where he had spent decades calling major NFL and MLB events. He moved to ESPN alongside longtime broadcast partner Troy Aikman, and the pair have continued working together on Monday Night Football.According to The Athletic’s Andrew Marchand, Buck’s new contract is worth around $108 million across six years. That works out to roughly $18 million per year, which would make him one of ESPN’s highest-paid on-air personalities. The Athletic was the first to report the agreement.The timing of the deal is important for ESPN. The network is preparing for its first Super Bowl broadcast in February 2027, with Buck and Aikman expected to be part of the main booth.

ESPN’s first Super Bowl will be Super Bowl LXI at SoFi Stadium in Los Angeles. ESPN has also secured the 2031 Super Bowl, meaning Buck’s new deal keeps him around for both major broadcasts.Buck’s current contract was set to expire after the 2026 NFL season. Before the reported extension, there had been plenty of interest in what he might do next, especially because his longtime partner Aikman’s contract situation was also uncertain.

Joe Buck had already made his ESPN retirement wish clear

The new agreement also fits with something Buck said earlier this year. In April, he spoke with Richard Deitsch of Sports Business Journal about his future and made it clear that he did not want to leave ESPN.“If you reached through my computer screen right now and handed me a contract to continue my time at ESPN, I would sign it without even looking at it,” Buck said.He added, “I’ve loved every second of it and I am hopeful that I’m at ESPN for the rest of my career.”At that time, Buck said there had been no formal contract talks, but he was open about wanting to remain with the network.Now, several months later, the reported six year agreement appears to give Buck exactly the kind of long-term future he had hoped for.

Buck is 57 and has already built one of the longest careers in sports broadcasting. He has called 24 World Series and has worked alongside Aikman for more than two decades. ESPN itself describes their partnership as the longest-running NFL broadcast duo in history.For now, the focus remains on Monday Night Football and ESPN’s first Super Bowl. Aikman’s future beyond the 2026 season is still separate from Buck’s new deal, but Buck’s place at ESPN is no longer nearly as uncertain as it was earlier this year.
